Architecture

[ART HOUSE]

Radical sculptural expressions - gallery and artist residence

Design Statement

The project concerns itself with a mode of exhibiting sculpture that departs entirely from the conventions of the gallery typology. Rooted in the sculptural tradition of Auguste Rodin, it proposes an architecture that serves as an extension of the artist's medium, where space itself becomes malleable - shaped to engage the visitor in an immediate encounter with form.

Sculpture, as a discipline, demands an architectural response that is neither neutral nor prescriptive. It requires a setting that amplifies its weight, texture, and capacity to inhabit space with a sense of corporeality. To achieve this, the gallery abandons the neutrality of the 'white cube' in favour of spaces that are sculptural in their own right - volumes modulated by light and shadow and tactile surfaces.

The programme, accommodating both exhibition and production, introduces a duality that is inherently productive. The presence of the artist within the fabric of the building ensures that the act of making is foregrounded, not relegated to an unseen process. The residency studio establishes a dialogue between creation and display.

Bristol, as a locus, offers a particular richness of context. Its industrial past, mercantile heritage, and contemporary vibrancy all provide an architectural narrative that resists simplification. The gallery doesn't impose itself as an alien object but catalyses a social regeneration in the west of Bristol.

Materiality is a matter of stylistic, ethical, and cultural significance. Rodin's work, with its raw surfaces and expressive power, suggests a palette of materials that speak directly to the hand of the maker. The materials, chosen for their resonance and durability, are sourced with an understanding of their origin, journey, and end.

The design's ambition is not merely to house sculpture but to offer a radical rethinking of how culture is encountered, shared, and understood. The building performs its environmental obligations with a quiet assurance, integrating passive systems and enduring materials to secure its place not only in the present but in the imagined future.
